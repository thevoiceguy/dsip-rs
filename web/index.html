This is your identity

DSIP has no accounts. Your identity is a key this browser just made; the DID below is its name, and it is what people call. Nothing about it is sent anywhere until you call or introduce yourself.

this browser; it signs for the identity under a delegation (§7.4)

The identity key exists only in this browser. Export it (Settings) and keep the file: without it, the identity is gone when this browser's storage is.

    The export is your identity key, encrypted with a passphrase you choose. Importing it on another browser enrols that browser as a device of this identity: it makes its own device key there, and your identity key signs the delegation (§7.4). No server is involved. Importing it here replaces this browser's device key.

    Devices

    A did:key identity has no document and no registry of its devices: this list is what this browser knows (itself, and the devices the identity file it was enrolled from listed). A device enrolled elsewhere appears here when you add its DID, shown on its own Settings screen. Revoking a device signs a delegation-revocation with the identity key (§7.4) and hands it to the relay, which ends that device's binding and refuses its next hello; it is held here too. A did:web identity would publish it in its document instead, where every verifier finds it.

        Policy

        Phone number

        A binding your carrier issued (Number Attestation Profile N§3): your calls present the number with it, and a callee verifies it against your identity's document. Your identity must be did:web and its document must list the number.

        Documents you hold

        DID documents of did:web identities, held here so their signatures verify (§8.1: a document is the authority for its DID; this browser does not fetch them). Paste one from its did.json.

          Contact link

          A link you hand to one person out of band (a message, a card). Opening it introduces them to you with a single-use token, and the introduction is granted at once without a request (§19.4). Each link works once.
